# v2.1 Improved Project Multiplier Analysis

**Status:** isolated shadow analysis. No production evaluator, official Accuracy artifact, calibration version, weights, fallback policy, raw DLD source, or historical result was changed.

## Why the first estimator was weakened

The first estimator compared each project's median price per square metre with the median of all other transactions in the same property type and district. That comparison was descriptive but vulnerable to composition bias. The diagnostics found that the training project and peer medians commonly differed by about 22 days, while apartment project cohorts sometimes differed materially in unit size and room mix. Several cohorts also had high within-project dispersion. The results explain why a broad `0.95–1.05` multiplier applied to most projects could move a baseline estimate in the wrong direction.

The diagnostic summary was:

| Type | Qualifying cohorts | Median absolute date gap | Median project/peer area ratio | High-dispersion cohorts | Candidates at bounds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Apartment | 757 | 22 days | 0.995 | 17 | 379 |
| Villa | 79 | 22 days | 0.991 | 0 | 14 |
| Townhouse | 0 | N/A | N/A | 0 | 0 |

## Estimator variants

The improved runner now tests four peer-relative variants: the legacy method, time-normalized peers, time-normalized peers matched by area and rooms, and a robust version with a higher project sample threshold, a neutral index band, and dispersion exclusion. It also tests an independent residual estimator based on verified Accuracy history:

> `Residual project signal = median(actualSalePrice ÷ existing aqarValuation)`

For the residual estimator, the signal is trained on earlier verified Accuracy records and applied to later verified Accuracy records. The resulting multiplier is shrunk toward `1.00`, bounded, and ignored for projects with insufficient evidence or high residual dispersion. A type-specific sensitivity test applies the residual signal to Villas only or Apartments only.

## Main holdout comparison

The main holdout begins on `2026-07-09`, with candidate signals trained on records before that date. All reported metrics use the same 1,108 verified residential Accuracy records.

| Scenario | Candidate coverage | Baseline MAE | Shadow MAE | Baseline P90 | Shadow P90 | Baseline ±15% | Shadow ±15% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Legacy peer-relative | 86.733% | 12.128% | 13.003% | 25.767% | 27.780% | 72.112% | 69.224% |
| Time-normalized and matched | 86.733% | 12.128% | 12.905% | 25.767% | 26.785% | 72.112% | 69.675% |
| Robust time/matched, n≥8 | 75.632% | 12.128% | 12.855% | 25.767% | 26.737% | 72.112% | 69.495% |
| Robust time/matched, n≥10 | 69.495% | 12.128% | 12.751% | 25.767% | 26.457% | 72.112% | 69.765% |
| Accuracy residual, all residential | 33.755% | 12.128% | 12.064% | 25.767% | 25.767% | 72.112% | 72.653% |
| Accuracy residual, Villas only | 3.159% | 12.128% | **12.030%** | 25.767% | **25.662%** | 72.112% | **72.834%** |
| Accuracy residual, Apartments only | 30.596% | 12.128% | 12.162% | 25.767% | 26.019% | 72.112% | 71.931% |

The residual estimator applied to Villas only is the strongest result in this run. It improves aggregate MAE by `0.098` percentage points, P90 by `0.105` percentage points, and ±15% coverage by `0.722` percentage points. The all-residential residual version improves MAE and ±15% modestly, but it is pulled down by Apartment performance. The Apartment-only experiment is negative on all three headline metrics.

The improvement is not yet sufficient for production activation. The Villa-only result affects only `35` of `125` Villa holdout records and only `3.159%` of the full residential holdout. Its bias moves from `2.318%` to `2.451%` in the aggregate. The selected estimator therefore remains a candidate, not an approved multiplier policy.

## Sensitivity of Villa multiplier bounds

The Villa-only residual signal was tested with symmetric maximum shifts around `1.00`:

| Maximum multiplier | Minimum multiplier | MAE | P90 | Within ±15% | Coverage |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.00× | 1.00× | 12.128% | 25.767% | 72.112% | 3.159% |
| 1.01× | 0.99× | 12.106% | 25.767% | 72.112% | 3.159% |
| 1.02× | 0.98× | 12.085% | 25.767% | 72.473% | 3.159% |
| 1.03× | 0.97× | 12.065% | 25.747% | 72.744% | 3.159% |
| 1.04× | 0.96× | 12.045% | 25.662% | 72.834% | 3.159% |
| 1.05× | 0.95× | **12.030%** | **25.662%** | **72.834%** | 3.159% |

The table shows a monotonic improvement in this particular holdout as the cap increases, but that is not evidence that `1.05×` is the correct production value. The upper cap should not be selected from the same holdout used to report the result. A further time period or rolling-origin validation is required.

## Decision and next safeguards

The improved estimator is better than the first peer-relative rule, especially when the signal is restricted to Villas and derived from verified historical residuals. However, the evidence is still limited by low coverage, no Townhouse holdout, residual dependence on the current evaluator, and the absence of an independent rolling validation period for cap selection.

The recommended next step is a rolling-origin experiment across multiple date windows, with the cap and neutral band selected only on an earlier validation window and reported on a later untouched window. The experiment should also report per-type results, project count, fallback unchanged count, multiplier distribution, bias direction, and confidence intervals or bootstrap ranges. Until that is complete, the Calibration Console remains able to store the candidate controls, but the shadow configuration remains disabled by default and the official value remains unchanged.

BUA/Plot Area and Last Renovation Year remain user-supplied scenario inputs. Their direct multipliers can be calculated by the isolated engine, but their coefficients cannot be fitted from the current DLD files because those files do not contain verified separate BUA, Plot Area, or renovation-year fields. No synthetic observations were introduced.
